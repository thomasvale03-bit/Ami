THE BASICS

1. What is Playmaker Entertainment?

Playmaker Entertainment is an independent nightlife promotions and entertainment company connecting guests with nightlife experiences, guest lists, tickets, and VIP table opportunities. We work with nightlife venues in Las Vegas and other markets, building relationships with venues, promoters, and customers.

Our goal is simple: generate real customers, real attendance, and revenue through a reliable promoter network.

Website: https://playmakerentertainment.com

2. What does a promoter do?

Promoters help Playmaker generate business by:

* Finding potential customers.
* Sharing approved Playmaker guest-list and ticket links.
* Registering clients under their own promoter attribution.
* Promoting approved nightlife venues and events.
* Generating table and ticket inquiries.
* Following up with customers before their night out.
* Making sure clients actually attend.
* Maintaining professional relationships with customers and venue staff.

The goal isn’t just collecting signups. It’s generating verified attendance and sales.

3. Is it a job, contract work, or commission only?

Playmaker operates with an independent promoter network.

Promoters work on a performance-based arrangement. Exact contractual classification, agreements, and onboarding paperwork must be confirmed by management before being represented as finalized policy.

GETTING CLIENTS SIGNED UP

13. How do clients sign up?

The official starting point is:

https://playmakerentertainment.com

The process should be:

1. Direct the customer to the appropriate venue or event page.
2. Have them select the correct event date.
3. Complete the guest-list registration.
4. Make sure the correct promoter is selected or credited.
5. Verify that the submission was completed.
6. Keep the customer informed about their registration.

Promoters should never register customers under another promoter’s name to manipulate credit.

For TAO-related venues, remember:

* Passes = Guest List.
* Tickets = Paid Tickets.

14. What’s the signup deadline?

Deadlines depend on the venue and event.

Do not advertise a guaranteed cutoff time unless it has been confirmed for that specific night.

Encourage customers to register as early as possible.

15. What information does a client need?

The exact required customer fields depend on the website’s registration form.

Promoters should be prepared to collect:

* Full name.
* Email address.
* Phone number, when requested.
* Event date.
* Venue.
* Number of guests.
* Gender breakdown, when required by the venue.
* Promoter attribution.

Never submit fabricated customer information.

16. What happens at the door?

Customers should follow the venue’s specific check-in instructions.

They should:

* Bring valid identification.
* Follow the venue’s entry instructions.
* Confirm their guest-list registration.
* Arrive within the applicable guest-list window.
* Be prepared to provide the name used during registration.

A Playmaker signup is not an unconditional guarantee of entry.

17. Age, ratios, and dress code

These requirements vary by venue and event.

Promoters should never assume that one venue’s rules apply to another.

Unless otherwise specified:

* Customers should expect nightclub age restrictions, generally 21+.
* Valid government-issued identification is important.
* Dress codes are determined by the individual venue.
* Guest-list gender ratios may apply.
* Management should verify specific requirements before they are communicated as guaranteed.

20. What’s the difference between guest lists, tickets, and tables?

Guest List:
Customers register for potential complimentary or venue-approved guest-list admission. Conditions, availability, and entry requirements apply.

Tickets:
Customers purchase admission through an authorized ticketing link. This is a paid admission option.

VIP Tables:
Customers purchase a reserved VIP experience, generally involving bottle service, minimum spends, and dedicated seating.

Promoters should:

* Offer guest lists to customers seeking guest-list admission.
* Offer tickets when customers want guaranteed ticketed admission under the ticket terms.
* Offer tables to groups looking for a VIP experience.

Never represent a guest-list signup as a paid ticket or guaranteed admission.

25. Can promoters use the Playmaker name and logo?

Yes, for authorized promotional purposes.

Promoters may identify themselves as Playmaker-affiliated promoters and share approved branding.

They may not:

* Claim to own Playmaker.
* Claim to represent a venue’s management.
* Create unauthorized partnerships.
* Alter official branding in a misleading way.

26. What should a first DM look like?

Example:

“Hey, what’s up? Are you planning on going out in Vegas this weekend? I work with Playmaker Entertainment and can help you with guest lists, tickets, or VIP tables at some of the top clubs. Let me know what you’re looking for.”

Keep conversations natural. Don’t make every interaction feel like a sales pitch.

27. What should promoters never do?

* Spam customers.
* Advertise fake promotions.
* Promise guaranteed free entry.
* Promise discounts without authorization.
* Misrepresent guest-list availability.
* Impersonate venue employees or management.
* Submit fake signups.
* Steal or manipulate another promoter’s credit.
* Use outdated flyers or event information.
* Make unauthorized financial promises.

Protect the Playmaker brand and customer relationships.

RULES AND CONDUCT

28. What gets a promoter removed?

Conduct that can lead to removal includes:

* Fraudulent signups.
* Manipulating attendance or commission records.
* Stealing another promoter’s customers.
* Misrepresenting Playmaker or venue relationships.
* Harassing customers or team members.
* Repeatedly ignoring management instructions.
* Unauthorized financial commitments.
* Conduct that damages Playmaker’s reputation.

Management makes final decisions regarding removal.

29. Can promoters work with other companies?

Playmaker’s exclusivity and conflict-of-interest policy needs management confirmation.

Promoters must never misrepresent another company’s business as Playmaker’s or misuse Playmaker’s customer data.

30. Rules about going out, drinking, and venue conduct

Promoters represent Playmaker when interacting with customers and venue personnel.

Expected conduct:

* Be respectful.
* Follow venue rules.
* Don’t create problems for venue staff.
* Don’t harass customers.
* Don’t make promises outside your authority.
* Maintain professional judgment.
* Don’t allow personal activities to interfere with promotional responsibilities.

31. What if a customer gets turned away?

Stay professional.

1. Ask what happened.
2. Find out which venue and date.
3. Get the customer’s name.
4. Determine whether the issue involved dress code, identification, capacity, timing, or another venue policy.
5. Notify management if necessary.

Never argue with door staff or promise reimbursement.

A guest-list registration does not override venue security or admission policies.

PROMOTER SELF-SIGNUP, SAME-DAY OMNIA, AND DASHBOARD

Approved policy as of November 27, 2026 (Tom Vale). Use this section when promoters ask about signing a guest up themselves, same-day OMNIA requests, or the promoter dashboard.

38. Can a promoter sign a guest up themselves?

Yes. Promoters can sign a guest up themselves through https://playmakerentertainment.com.

1. Select OMNIA and the event date.
2. Enter the guest’s contact information so the guest can receive their TAO pass.
3. Put your own first name in the promoter first-name field.

The website fills in the request automatically. The promoter gets credit when the guest checks in under their referral. Being counted for the guest list still does not guarantee check-in credit.

39. How should same-day OMNIA requests be handled?

The rules depend on the night.

Friday: The guest list is closed. Decline the same-day request.

Saturday: The guest list is closed. Decline the OMNIA request and reroute the guest to another nightclub.

If a guest still insists on OMNIA guest-list placement on Saturday, explain the fee before collecting it, using this exact wording:

“The promoter fee is $25 per person for guest-list placement. Entry is still up to the venue and isn’t guaranteed.”

This $25 per person promoter fee is an approved guest-list placement fee, not a promoter payout or commission. Jacob may quote this exact fee in this Saturday insist case only. Do not share any other dollar amounts, rates, or payouts. Do not use skip-the-line wording.

40. Is there a promoter dashboard?

Yes. Promoters have a promoter dashboard where they can see their activity for the day and see when customer reviews come in.
